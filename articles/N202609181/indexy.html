Three more Indian cars criticised for weak rear seatbelt reminders in latest #WhatTheBeep evaluations

18.09.2026



Today, The Yawning Chihuahua is releasing #WhatTheBeep evaluation results for the rear seatbelt reminders of three Indian cars: the Kia CARENS CLAVIS EV, Hyundai AURA, and Toyota INNOVA HYCROSS.

Kia Carens Clavis EV's rear seat conveniences overshadowed by a suboptimal seatbelt reminder

Launched last year, the rear seats of the Kia CARENS CLAVIS EV pamper its rear passengers with sun blinds, a tray table, and an air purifier. However, little thought has been given to its seatbelt reminder, which only chimes once a rear seatbelt is taken off while driving, the bare minimum regulations require.

With no occupant detection, the CARENS CLAVIS EV fails to audibly alert unbelted rear occupants unless they've unfastened a fastened belt. It fails two of the four #WhatTheBeep testcases, and is awarded an overall FAIL.


Toyota Innova Hycross' incredibly persistent chimes don't start when needed most

During testing of the Toyota INNOVA HYCROSS, it was revealed that the audible signal of its rear seatbelt reminder uses a more advanced kind of second-level signal, split into an intermediate warning followed by a very persistent final warning.

It is a disappointment, however, that this advanced warning does not activate when needed most: to remind rear occupants who simply haven't buckled up. Since the INNOVA HYCROSS cuts out occupant detection, its rear seatbelt reminder is forced to ding only when a belt changes status to unfastened, if it has to avoid false alerts. This behaviour earns it a FAIL in #WhatTheBeep.


Hyundai's sedans continue string of disappointing #WhatTheBeep results

The Hyundai AURA is a popular taxi. Tested in its Prime SD guise launched last December, the affordable sedan also receives FAIL, with no occupant detection and a chime only on change of belt status.


The Aura's larger stablemate, the Hyundai VERNA, was originally awarded FAIL in a #WhatTheBeep evaluation based on desktop research earlier this year, which has now been audited via in-person testing. The Yawning Chihuahua is pleased to report that all testcase outcomes agree with the original result.
